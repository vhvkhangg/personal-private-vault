## 3. Findings and independently examined counterexamples

### P17-CF-1 — High: V does not preserve per-source consent history

**Evidence:** proposal lines 109–116 replace the original combined authorization epoch with `(RAG epoch, V)`. Lines 154–164 make V global and intra-attempt, explicitly avoiding invalidation of otherwise valid approvals after unrelated changes. Owner changes advance V without synchronously changing the RAG source epoch; reconciliation is eventual. Lines 329–336 nevertheless require renewed approval for changed sources. Accepted P17-3 §1 requires a monotonic authorization epoch for NSFW/trash transitions and prohibits treating events as permission.

The following is a **design counterexample**, not an enabled-runtime exploit:

| Step | Durable owner truth | RAG approval | Global V | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | A allowed, source revision h | Approved at epoch e, fields f, runtime r | 0 | Initial approval is valid. |
| 1 | Owner COMMIT sets A NSFW | Still approved at e; notification lost | 1 | Old V=0 tickets reject. Current A is ineligible. |
| 2 | Owner COMMIT sets A allowed again, with original selected fields/content | Still approved at e; second notification lost | 2 | Current projection can match h again. No renewed consent occurred. |
| 3 | New admission/J reads fresh A | Same e/f/r and h | 2 | The specified current-state predicates can pass, minting a **new** V=2 ticket using old consent. Old-ticket rejection did not solve this. |

There is an information-loss problem: for A, this final observation is indistinguishable from two unrelated participating mutations that advanced V to 2 while A never became ineligible. The proposal intends to preserve A's approval in that unrelated case. A global counter plus current A state cannot distinguish the two histories without additional retained information. Hashing the current NSFW boolean would still return the original value after the round trip.

Current source inspection supports, rather than removes, this concern. [RagSourceAuthority](../../../../backend/src/main/java/com/vhvkhangg/personalprivatevault/rag/internal/application/RagSourceAuthority.java) checks current eligibility, approval instance, fields and revision at lines 98–126. [RagSource.approve/revoke](../../../../backend/src/main/java/com/vhvkhangg/personalprivatevault/rag/internal/domain/RagSource.java) advances the RAG epoch on its own transitions. The [Fiction hash builder](../../../../backend/src/main/java/com/vhvkhangg/personalprivatevault/fiction/internal/application/FictionSourceService.java) and [Film hash builder](../../../../backend/src/main/java/com/vhvkhangg/personalprivatevault/film/internal/application/FilmSourceService.java) hash current selected values plus Vault lifecycle `updatedAt`, not a monotonic Fiction/Film mutation generation. Their owner-local source records omit the domain update timestamp. [FictionService.update](../../../../backend/src/main/java/com/vhvkhangg/personalprivatevault/fiction/internal/application/FictionService.java), lines 151–204, takes the Vault guard and updates Fiction; [VaultEntryMutationGuard](../../../../backend/src/main/java/com/vhvkhangg/personalprivatevault/vault/internal/application/entry/VaultEntryMutationGuard.java) only locks/refreshes and does not advance lifecycle state or consent. Therefore Vault lifecycle hashing does not supply NSFW mutation history. No v3 history mechanism is specified to replace this gap.

**Smallest required design correction:** choose and specify a durable, monotonic source-affecting mutation generation, captured by explicit approval and advanced atomically with every relevant owning-source mutation, or an equivalent durable invalidation/history scheme that survives lost notifications and rollback. It must cover transitive children and affected approval fields. Another safe but less available option is to invalidate approvals conservatively on **every** V change, including unrelated changes; that contradicts the draft's unrelated-approval promise and needs explicit owner acceptance. Do not solve this with wall-clock timestamps, eventual events, current-value hashes or an unapproved owner-to-RAG dependency.

**Acceptance:** deterministic real owner API tests for allowed → NSFW → allowed, content h → h2 → h, assignment removal/restoration and relevant trash/restore transitions, with both notifications dropped and no intervening RAG read. Fresh admission **and** J must deny under old consent, as must active/history reads that require renewed authority; explicit reapproval may admit a new attempt. Include the corresponding child-source and unrelated-source cases, rollback, concurrent approval and restart/restore. Preserve the chosen unrelated-approval behavior explicitly.

The review-only `consent_model.py` executed five assertions about the draft predicates: old tickets reject after either mutation, while new-ticket eligibility can still pass under old consent; the unrelated-history observation matches. Exit 0 means the **counterexample was found**. It is not a security regression PASS or a test of production code. Containment currently denies execution regardless of these predicates.

### P17-CF-2 — High: transaction entry must make lock order enforceable

**Evidence:** proposal lines 263–282 prescribe a global order and reject ambient transactions with unprovable earlier locks, but do not define the public precondition/enforcement boundary for all owner and RAG entry points. Current owner `update` methods can join a caller transaction; existing `acquireSourceGuard` is a mandatory caller-transaction row guard, not proof that no earlier lock/flush occurred.

Counterexample if the new guard is inserted only at today's guard call: transaction X already holds Vault row R, or has flushed an owner change; child Y holds shared F, and J waits for R; X then requests exclusive F. X waits for Y, while Y waits for J/X. This is a cross-OS/SQL cycle that PostgreSQL's deadlock detector cannot resolve as a complete graph. A timeout can bound failure but does not establish correct composition. A caller that already holds the graph barrier and later asks for V similarly inverts V → graph. Suspending a shared-F outer transaction for an exclusive-F `REQUIRES_NEW` mutation can self-block even on one Java thread.

**Required revision:** specify a supported physical-transaction ownership protocol. Either participating outer transaction entry acquires the correct F/V mode before **any** managed mutation, flush or later-order lock, or calls from an unregistered/already-active transaction are rejected before side effects. Reuse must be bound to physical connection/transaction identity, not a method, thread boolean or logical nested scope. Define same-mode nesting, prohibited mode upgrades/suspension, savepoint rules and unknown completion. Do not add a generic framework or independently committing business writes to hide this issue.

Spring distinguishes REQUIRED logical scopes from their shared physical transaction; REQUIRES_NEW retains outer resources while using an independent transaction, and NESTED uses savepoints. The proposal must be proven against the actual configured manager/proxies, including self-invocation and auto-flush. [Spring transaction propagation](https://docs.spring.io/spring-framework/reference/data-access/transaction/declarative/tx-propagation.html).

F may be released after confirmed physical completion, never on service return, `beforeCompletion` or savepoint rollback. An unknown result is not committed authorization. `afterCompletion` has committed/rolled-back/unknown statuses; callbacks do not replace durable ticket/receipt recovery. No controller waiting for a child may retain a DB lock the child needs. [Spring synchronization lifecycle](https://docs.spring.io/spring-framework/docs/current/javadoc-api/org/springframework/transaction/support/TransactionSynchronization.html).

**Acceptance:** reject ambient execution before prepare/spawn/body; exercise owner calls inside clean registered and dirty/unregistered outer transactions, cached/dirty managed entities, same/opposite-mode nesting, suspended transactions, savepoint rollback, outer rollback, timeout, process death and unknown connection completion. Prove no mutation/child/ticket side effect on rejection and no order inversion across public, indirect, bulk/import or cleanup paths. Inventory every participant, rather than assuming the 13 previously tested paths exhaust all future entry points.

### P17-CF-3 — Medium: specify exact SQL and one-use state transitions

**Evidence:** proposal lines 170–193 and 213–249 correctly require same-transaction V/attempt guards, but leave the concrete meaning of shared V and the durable G transition implicit. ADMITTED → PREPARED → TRANSFER_AUTHORIZED → STAGED does not identify the consumed/reserved intermediate state.

Require V readers to obtain an actual conflicting row lock, such as `FOR SHARE`, and V mutators to obtain an incompatible lock/update before later locks. `FOR KEY SHARE` is insufficient against a non-key V increment: it permits `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`. A plain SELECT also provides no writer exclusion. Compare expected V **after the locking read has completed**, on the same physical transaction/connection retained through S; never compare first and lock later. Savepoint release/rollback must not drop the only guard protecting a surviving write. [PostgreSQL row-lock modes](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/explicit-locking.html).

Specify a durable compare-and-set reservation at G and an atomic, child/boot/nonce/attempt-bound one-use body-permit consumption before any body read. Two serial G requests must not each see an unused ticket and issue permits. Unknown G COMMIT cannot cause retransmission; the safe action is to abort/query bounded metadata, then use a new admitted attempt if necessary. Repeated S may return only the **same exact committed receipt**, never overwrite stage/digests or renew expiry. An A received from the wire is insufficient until its scoped durable receipt is verified. These are state transitions within the existing canonical job engine, not a second job scheduler.

Require every old function/trigger/view/DML route to follow V → attempt or be inaccessible, including legacy v2 helpers, cleanup, deferred constraints and direct worker DML. The worker must not choose expected V, access the Vault function directly, acquire business locks or modify authority. Audited definer identity, fixed safe search path, grants, table ownership and effective role inheritance must be reviewed together.

**Acceptance:** actual-role conflicting-lock tests and server-side COMMIT barriers in both orders; attempts at KEY SHARE/plain-read substitutes must fail the test; duplicate/concurrent G/body/S/A requests, delayed SQL, unknown G/S COMMIT and different-result receipt replay must deny or return the exact prior source-free outcome. Verify V and attempt locks survive until physical transaction completion and no legacy route bypasses them.

### P17-CF-4 — High: D6 needs a precise stage boundary and explicit owner semantics

**Evidence:** proposal lines 213–232 issue ALLOW_BODY after G COMMIT, then verify a permit before reading; lines 340–358 distinguish stage-boundary expiry from instantaneous interruption. Accepted P17-3 §3.2 already describes stage-boundary checks, but also confines Python to leased preapproved input; §2 says session expiry stops reads. Neither authorizes a reusable or indefinitely delayed permit.

Trace: G passes with two seconds of PG-reported remaining validity; its response or the child is paused for longer than that validity; the child resumes before its first body read. A permit-only body route can accept an expired authorization. Separately, pausing between any successful chunk check and the next CPU read can produce a post-expiry read even when per-chunk checks exist. S/P denial prevents persistence/publication but does not undo that exposure. F excludes owner/policy COMMIT; **time passing does not acquire F**, so the NSFW proof does not prove continuous lease validity.

**Recommendation:** retain stage-boundary execution semantics, subject to explicit owner confirmation and revised text. Define G reservation versus actual body acceptance, require current unexpired lease/session and the remaining operation budget at the body-start gate, reject delayed/expired permits before application body reads, check cancellation and duration at every bounded read/work unit, and abort/discard on expiry. Derive any local monotonic deadline conservatively from the PG observation plus elapsed request time; never reset it after a pause/handshake, subtract unrelated wall clocks, extend TTL, or give the original two-second budget again at each phase. Full-unit remaining lifetime at G must include elapsed F/J time and cannot supersede the earlier F deadline.

The accepted post-lock-wait and outer-transaction expiry regressions at S/P must remain. Recheck PG time after every relevant wait and in deferred SQL deadline guards; retain V/attempt/source locks through physical COMMIT. Precisely state the expiry comparison's SQL linearization point: a deferred trigger runs during commit processing, not an oracle measuring the later WAL flush/client acknowledgement. A pause after its last time check is another check-to-action window. Do not claim a literal timestamp bound on every final CPU instruction or COMMIT acknowledgement without a stronger mechanism and evidence. This does not authorize weakening existing deadline tests. [Deferred constraint-trigger timing](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/sql-createtrigger.html).

Also test `SET CONSTRAINTS ... IMMEDIATE` as each effective runtime role: PostgreSQL permits deferred triggers to be fired earlier. Hypothetical trace: force the deadline trigger immediate, insert a receipt while valid, keep the transaction open across expiry, then COMMIT. An early trigger check alone is not end-of-transaction expiry enforcement. Whether a permitted current route can realize this trace is **NOT_VERIFIED**, not a newly reproduced implementation finding; the v3 SQL contract must exclude or safely handle it.

**Required owner decision:** accept the explicitly bounded stage/gate interpretation and its suspension residual, or require continuous zero-read-after-expiry semantics. If the latter remains mandatory, this protocol is insufficient and containment remains until another requirement/design is approved. This review grants neither a grace period nor a relaxation of NSFW-COMMIT, revocation/purge ordering, expired pre-body admission or SQL deadline gates.

**Acceptance:** independently delay G before commit, G response, body acceptance and each chunk; expire the independent session and attempt separately, including equality and post-lock waits; test pauses across the deadline and shifted JVM/Python wall clocks. Expected reads, aborts, receipts and P outcomes must be specified per gate. Pending S/P COMMIT after the last deferred check needs an explicit documented limit rather than an unexecuted absolute guarantee.

## 4. Adversarial interleaving assessment

`N` means physical exclusion/mutation COMMIT; `C` means consumer application reads/work; `J/G/S/P` use the proposal's definitions. **Logical prevention** below is conditional reasoning, not executed runtime proof. All new native/v3 guarantees remain NOT_VERIFIED.

| Case | Interleaving and result |
| --- | --- |
| Owner NSFW commits before consumption | N completes under exclusive F/V; child then obtains shared F; fresh J observes ineligibility and sends no body. Logically prevented, subject to fresh owner proof and CF-1 for round trips. |
| Owner NSFW requested during consumption | Child holds shared F; owner cannot obtain exclusive F or commit N until child death. C precedes N, even if Java dies. Logically prevented if the actual consumer retains its own F throughout all threads' access. |
| Owner NSFW commits after consumption/exit | N may advance V before S/P. Already consumed input preceded N; delayed S/P must reject or have committed before N. Later history/active reads require fresh authority and consent history. |
| Mutation bypasses F or V | C starts, unguarded owner commits N, C resumes. Unsafe. D1's disable-unverified-writers rule is essential; a path inventory and supported-writer deployment check are required, not events. |
| Ambient owner holds R before F | Child shared F/J waits R; owner waits exclusive F. Cross-resource cycle; CF-2 revision required. |
| Graph/attempt-first legacy route | X holds graph/attempt and waits V; Y holds V and waits that graph/attempt. Inversion; revoke/adapt every route and reject incompatible ambient calls. |
| Shared-to-exclusive upgrade or REQUIRES_NEW | Outer shared F remains held while inner wants exclusive F; progress can stop. Refuse before side effects; no timed retry that preserves old proof. |
| Savepoint rollback | Rolling back the subtransaction that acquired SQL guards can release them while the outer transaction survives. Do not allow a surviving dependent write without its guards; reacquire in canonical order or abort the whole protected unit. |
| Spring rollback/failed J | Method returns or flush succeeds, but outer/physical J rolls back. No committed ticket means no body authorization. Execution must own its short J and reject ambient execution before prepare. |
| Java death or connection loss during T/C | Ordinary source DB locks can disappear; child's independent shared F remains. N blocks or fails boundedly. Parent death must not close/unlock the child's handle or transfer content into a surviving unfenced helper. |
| Owner Java dies during pending N COMMIT | Exclusive F may release before the server finishes N. V exclusive already held by that same owner TX must block fresh J/G until N commits/aborts; fresh post-lock reads then decide. Add this symmetric test, not only the worker-S death test. |
| Worker death during pending S COMMIT | Child dies and F releases while DB backend still runs S. S's V shared blocks N; otherwise N increments V before S acquires it and stale S rejects. This is why F alone is insufficient. |
| Worker DB loss while child remains alive | Unknown S result is not A. F remains; terminate/reap child, then query durable scoped receipt and drain writer state. Never infer rollback from disconnect or resend input. |
| Delayed/duplicated SQL receipts | Server request queued before N must check current V after locking; stale queued S rejects. Duplicate receipt must match the exact existing tuple and cannot create new authority. CF-3 specifies transitions. |
| Failed process termination | A successful termination request alone does not prove death. Until the actual process handle signals and SQL writer state is drained, no slot release/quiescence/purge completion. Continue denial/busy on uncertainty. |
| Long exclusive owner wait | Readers may continue while an exclusive waiter exists; no fairness assumption. Each request fails within its own deadline, but eventual owner success is not guaranteed. A safe admission-stop/drain policy and availability decision are needed; never break F. |
| Cross-process/cross-session Windows locks | Same file identity/range and incompatible modes must block across actual Java/Python processes/sessions. Java FileLock, a parent-held lock, process naming or a boolean cannot substitute. Native proof outstanding. |

Unsafe unless stable installation-bound identity, protected namespace/handles and fail-closed bootstrap prevent the split. No automatic recreation while live consumers may exist. |
| Handle inheritance/early close | Closing a locking handle can release its lock while the process survives; inheriting a file handle is not proof that the child owns the required lock. Child acquires/owns its guard; no early unlock/close or hidden consumer descendants. Native cleanup tests outstanding. |
| Lease expires during body | F does not stop the clock. Per-unit abort and S/P denial are plausible stage semantics; zero post-expiry CPU reads are not proved. CF-4 and explicit owner decision required. |

## 5. Deadlocks, physical transactions and alternative designs

The proposed total order is reasonable: F mode → V mode → graph → sorted Vault IDs → sorted RAG source IDs → attempts → generations → runs. Owner writers omit RAG locks; worker SQL omits owner/graph locks. Cleanup without F is safe only after verified consumer death, confined to V → attempt with no later acquisition of F/graph/source guards; full purge is a separate ordered unit. This avoids a cleanup back-edge. The order must include implicit ORM flushes, FK/trigger locks, callbacks, recovery, bulk paths and cross-source operations, not just explicitly named application calls.

The V barrier has a valid purpose after either process dies. For S first, retained V shared prevents N's exclusive V until S resolves. For N first, the changed V invalidates queued S. For a dying owner, exclusive V similarly orders a new J after the still-pending owner transaction. These proofs require the actual conflicting SQL lock, post-wait fresh reads, atomic version increment with owner truth, complete participation and no bypass through legacy SQL. A bare global counter read does not provide this ordering.

| Design | Feasibility and trade-off |
| --- | --- |
| F + V + one short-lived consumer | Plausible for the proposed one-Windows-host profile. Preserves no body/model work under DB guards. Requires native identity/lifetime proof, broader owner metadata/API/SQL approval, revised consent history, full transaction participation and acceptance of global contention/busy failures. Not yet a demonstrated FR17-5 closure. |
| Prepared PostgreSQL source-guard reservation | A viable alternative: prepare a guard-only transaction on the complete source/graph/policy authority before transfer; retain it until consumer death **and** all possible attempt writers are sealed/drained. Do not hold attempt rows needed by S. Separate visible admission/ticket state is needed because prepared writes are not yet committed. Requires a durable coordinator, bounded prepared slots, crash/startup recovery and reviewed Spring/JPA composition; ordinary callback-level PREPARE is not a supported transaction protocol. |
| Ordinary SQL guards plus send/ACK | Fails if Java connection loss/timeout releases guards while Python still holds input. Adding callbacks, short TTL, another pre-send SELECT or a final publication check does not close that interval. |
| F alone | Stops N while the child can read, but a server-side S can finish after child death releases F. Requires a SQL writer barrier such as V/attempt fencing. |
| V/epoch alone | Rejects stale SQL outcomes, but cannot stop an already buffered live consumer from reading after owner N. Durable history solves consent renewal, not consumer lifetime. |
| Pipe/mmap/encryption/immutable staging alone | Transport/storage changes do not revoke a buffered copy or a delayed decryption key after guards disappear. Staging also adds raw-retention/purge obligations. A genuinely separate owner-approved snapshot changes source semantics and is not the current live-source contract. |
| Java-only/no-content-to-Python profile | Could remove this particular Python consumption boundary, but changes the approved end-to-end foundation behavior; it needs explicit functional re-scoping. It must not be credited as successful verification of the current Python-content workflow. |
| Retain containment | Safe current state while contracts are unresolved. This does not satisfy enabled-runtime FR17-5/8/9/10 acceptance, but avoids accepting an unproved synchronization protocol. |

Prepared transactions retain locks independently of the creating session and can be resolved by another session. They need timely coordinator recovery; lingering prepared work can obstruct maintenance/VACUUM. This is not technically impossible, but it moves failure handling into a durable distributed transaction protocol. [PostgreSQL PREPARE TRANSACTION](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/sql-prepare-transaction.html).

They are exempt from `transaction_timeout`; lock timeout is a per-lock wait bound, not an end-to-end operation budget. Thus prepared reservations would require an explicit exception to current bounded DB-guard lifetimes and the no-network-under-source-DB-guards rule, plus a safe unresolved-reservation policy. Reaping by age while a consumer survives would recreate the original race. Neither prepared transactions nor F/V resolves CF-4's instantaneous clock-interruption question. [PostgreSQL client timeouts](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/runtime-config-client.html).

## 7. Resources, availability and residual limitations

Global F serializes all participating mutations against content consumers, including unrelated sources. Global V serializes writer changes and invalidates outstanding tickets on unrelated participating mutations. After child exit, an unrelated V increment can also cause P to reject completed work; this is conservative safety with retry/availability cost. A long-running owner transaction can hold F exclusively until its actual completion, and a lost connection can leave V waits after F release. One active consumer limits throughput but does not ensure writer fairness or bounded eventual success.

The proposed 2s F threshold, 5s startup, 250ms control/cooperative grace, 1s death wait, 30s queue age, 256 MiB combined Python/128 MiB child caps and 16 MiB incremental buffer target are **unmeasured proposals**. Distinguish hard enforced limits from buffer measurement targets and normal-operation acceptance thresholds. Timer expiry cannot safely force-release a live child. Per-operation deadlines must share one elapsed budget; per-lock/per-phase resets could otherwise multiply worst-case latency. Startup happens before F but consumes the sole active slot; queue-inclusive latency must include it. Sixteen queued items need not all succeed within 30s, but the accepted workload must have a meaningful success/failure target so a fast-denial-only benchmark cannot pass.

Approve or revise operating thresholds only after representative supported-host measurements: normal successful work at 100 selected sources/depth 5/near-1-MiB plus 16-item saturation, below/at/over bounds, independent writers and isolated F/V/row waits. Record p50/p95, maxima/outliers, accepted/rejected/completed counts, cancellation/revoke/purge latency, CPU/private commit/JVM memory, handles/processes/DB connections and zero scratch/GPU use. Bounded safe failures under induced stalls are distinct from normal successful capacity. A source-free admission-stop/drain policy should prioritize pending revoke/recovery and avoid continuous fresh readers defeating repeated owner operations; no fairness guarantee may be invented.
